re-run the figures update and these captions must be checked against the new

One figure is not regenerated: Figure 1 is the location map of the first
submission (`fig_study_area_published.jpg`), carried over unchanged. It shows

Artwork conforms to the Elsevier specification: 90 mm single column, 190 mm

## Main text

### Figure 1 — `fig_study_area_published`

Study area. Taipei City, its twelve administrative districts and its setting in
northern Taiwan. Unchanged from the first submission. `composite_check` in the
same folder is a true-colour Sentinel-2 median composite of the same extent,
generated by `fig_maps.py` as a check that the exported bands are intact; it is
not a manuscript figure.

### Figure 2 — `fig_workflow`

Methodology. Processing runs top to bottom through six stages: image and
vector input, compositing and MultiPolygon explosion, polygon-grouped
sampling, tuning and classification, accuracy and area assessment, and output.
Counts inside the boxes are read from the analysis outputs rather than typed
in. The dashed gold edge marks the one feedback path in the design: the
stratum weights *W<sub>i</sub>* of the reference sample are the mapped class
areas, so the reference sample cannot be drawn until each model's map exists,
and each model is therefore assessed against a sample stratified by its own
map. 1.5 column.

### Figure 3 — `fig_classification_scheme`

Classification scheme. (a) The per-pixel feature vector: four Sentinel-2
surface reflectance bands, B2, B3, B4 and B8, scaled to 0–1, labelled by the
`Id` property of the polygon the pixel falls in. (b) Why every split is
grouped by polygon. Pixels drawn from one training polygon are near-duplicates
of one another; a pixel-level split places copies of the same observation on
both sides of the divide, so the hold-out score measures memorisation rather
than generalisation. The 70/30 hold-out and the five tuning folds both keep
each polygon whole. (c) The six classifiers, which share features, split and
tuning budget — the only difference between the six maps is the decision rule.
Double column.

### Figure 4 — `fig_lulc_panel`

Land use and land cover of Taipei City in 2020 as mapped by each of the six
classifiers, at 10 m in EPSG:32651. The colours are those of the Earth Engine
`palette` argument used throughout the study: Water `#1200ff`, Built-up
`#ff0000`, Forest/Vegetation `#2a7f00`, Plantation `#96b058`, Swamp `#04d1ff`.
Panels are ordered by hold-out overall accuracy, which is printed with each
model name; the mapped built-up area is annotated at the lower right of each
panel. Scale bar and north arrow apply to all six panels. Double column,
300 dpi.

### Figure 5 — `fig_lulc_detail`

Land use and land cover of Taipei City in 2020 from the most accurate of the
six classifiers, SVM with an RBF kernel (hold-out overall accuracy 0.956), at
full page. The twelve administrative districts are named and their boundaries
drawn in white over the classification. Three windows are enlarged in the
margins, each outlined on the main map at the same letter: (A) the Beitou and
Shilin foothills, where the forest–plantation boundary runs along the terrain
break; (B) the Zhongshan–Datong–Songshan core, the densest built-up fabric in
the city; (C) the Wenshan valley, where built-up land follows the tributaries
into forest. Palette as in Figure 4. Raster warped to EPSG:4326 for display so
that north is true north across the whole sheet; classification was carried out
at 10 m in EPSG:32651. Double column, 300 dpi.

Panel B shows the one systematic failure that survives in the best model: the
wide boulevards of the basin floor are assigned to Water and Swamp rather than
Built-up. With only B2, B3, B4 and B8 available, deep street canyons in
building shadow have the low reflectance across all four bands that defines
those classes, and Figure S4 shows Built-up against Swamp is the one class pair
whose Jeffries–Matusita distance falls short of separability at 1.848. This is
the same defect that Figures 10(a) and 10(c) quantify, seen directly.

### Figure 6 — `fig_accuracy_ci`

Model accuracy with uncertainty. (a) Hold-out overall accuracy and (b) hold-out
kappa, with 2000-replicate bootstrap 95% confidence intervals over the 1829
validation pixels. (c) Area-weighted overall accuracy after Olofsson et al.
(2014), with the interval computed on the effective sample size that remains
once the polygon design effect is applied; *n*<sub>eff</sub> is annotated for
each model. The contrast between (a) and (c) is the point of the figure: the
hold-out intervals
in (a) treat the validation pixels as independent, which the polygon design
does not license, and the apparent ranking of the six models lives entirely
inside that unjustified precision. In (c) the 1829 pixels are worth between 43
and 73 independent observations, and every interval covers every other model's
estimate. Double column.

### Figure 7 — `fig_mcnemar`

Pairwise significance. (a) Holm-adjusted McNemar *p*-values for all fifteen
model pairs; the lower triangle is the standard pixel-level test, the upper
triangle the same comparison with the polygon-cluster correction of Durkalski
et al. (2003). Colour is on a log scale. (b) The same fifteen comparisons as a
paired dot plot, naive against cluster-corrected, with the 0.05 threshold
marked. Eight of the fifteen differences are significant when the validation
pixels are treated as independent; none survives the correction for their
clustering in 30 polygons. Double column.

### Figure 8 — `fig_area_uncertainty`

Between-model area uncertainty. (a) Mapped area of each class in each of the
six classified maps, one dot per model on the grey range bar, annotated with
the ratio of the largest to the smallest estimate. (b) Design-based area
estimates with 95% confidence intervals, computed per model after Olofsson et
al. (2014) using that model's own map as the stratification. (c) The
comparison that matters, for each class: the red bar is half the between-model
range from panel (a), i.e. the plus-or-minus that model choice alone
contributes; the blue bar is the median across the six models of the
half-width of the single-model 95% interval from panel (b), i.e. the
plus-or-minus that sampling contributes. The annotated ratio is red divided by
blue. For Built-up (2.0×), Swamp (1.5×) and Water (1.1×) the choice of
classifier moves the area estimate further than that classifier's own
confidence interval admits, so an area quoted from one model with its own
interval understates the real uncertainty. Swamp differs by a factor of three
between the most and least generous model. Double column.

### Figure 9 — `fig_sampling_design`

Sampling design and effective sample size. (a) The 110 training polygons
across the city, 80 assigned to training and 30 to validation; ring size is
proportional to the number of 10 m pixels the polygon contributes. (b) Cluster
size, i.e. pixels per polygon, by class and split, on a log scale, with the
median marked. (c) What the clustering costs. Starting from the full
validation set, pixels are thinned within polygons (red, every polygon kept)
or whole polygons are removed (blue, every pixel of the surviving polygons
kept), and the effective sample size *n*<sub>eff</sub> = *n*/deff is
recomputed, with deff = 1 + (*m*<sub>0</sub> − 1)·ICC and the
intra-cluster correlation held at its full-sample estimate. Discarding half
the validation pixels costs almost no information; discarding half the
polygons costs about half of it. (d) The design effect class by class and
model by model, *n*<sub>eff,i</sub> against *n*<sub>i</sub>, with 1:1, 10× and
100× reference lines. Double column, 300 dpi.

### Figure 10 — `fig_error_concentration`

Where the misclassification is. (a) The recurring confusions: for each ordered
class pair, the median across the six models of the share of the true class
assigned to the other, with the median pixel count and the number of models
affected. (b) For each class and model, the share of that class's validation
errors that falls inside its single worst polygon; the horizontal line is the
median across models and the polygon count for each class is printed above.
(c, d) Position of each validation polygon on the axis joining the two class
centroids in the z-scored four-band feature space, where 0 is the centre of
the labelled class and 1 the centre of the class it is confused with; marker
size is proportional to the polygon's pixel count and polygons past the
halfway line are labelled. Errors are not scattered: two class pairs account
for nearly all of them, within a class they concentrate in one or two
polygons, and those polygons sit at or beyond the halfway point of the
centroid axis, which is the signature of overlapping class definitions rather
than of mislabelled reference data. Double column.

## Supplementary

### Figure S1 — `fig_lulc_best` (superseded, not submitted)

The highest-accuracy map, SVM with an RBF kernel, at larger scale, with the
mapped area of each class alongside. Same palette and projection as Figure 4.
Figure 5 now shows the same map at full page with district names and zoom
insets, so this one is redundant; `fig_maps.py` still writes it as a quick
check that the per-model GeoTIFFs mosaicked correctly. Leave it out of the
submission.

### Figure S2 — `fig_model_agreement`

Spatial structure of the disagreement between models. Left: for every pixel,
the number of the six classifiers that assign it the modal class, from 1
(complete disagreement) to 6 (unanimous). Right: for each modal class, the
share of its pixels at each agreement level. All six models agree on 81% of
the city, but the remainder is not distributed evenly: 94% of the pixels
mapped as Forest/Vegetation are unanimous against only 50% of those mapped as
Swamp and 64% of Plantation. The disagreement is spatially organised, forming
narrow bands along class boundaries and concentrating in the basin rather than
scattering as independent noise would. Double column, 300 dpi.

### Figure S3 — `fig_perclass_ua_pa`

Per-class user's (a) and producer's (b) accuracy for each model, with 95%
confidence intervals from the area-weighted estimator. Class colours behind
each group are those of Figure 4.

### Figure S4 — `fig_separability`

Jeffries–Matusita distance between every pair of classes in the four-band
feature space, computed on the training pixels. The measure is bounded above
by 2, and pairs below roughly 1.9 are conventionally read as not reliably
separable. Only Built-up versus Swamp falls short, at 1.848; every other pair
exceeds 1.98. This is the feature-space explanation for the concentration seen
in Figure 10 — the misclassification is a property of the band set, not of the
classifiers.

### Figure S5 — `fig_reference_design`

Design of the blind reference sample. (a) Stratum weights *W<sub>i</sub>*,
i.e. each class's share of the mapped area. (b) Sample allocation: strictly
proportional allocation in grey against the allocation used, which raises each
rare class to a floor of 75 points. (c) What the floor buys, as the 95%
half-width of the user's accuracy estimate at the prior accuracy assumed for
each class. Proportional allocation would leave Plantation with 35 points and
a user's accuracy no better than about ±16 percentage points.

## Notes for the response letter

Three figures do work that no table can do, and are worth pointing the
reviewers at explicitly:

Figure 6 panel (c) against panel (a) answers the objection that 1829
validation pixels ought to give precise accuracies. Figure 7 shows that the
statistical separation between the models reported in the original submission
was an artefact of treating those pixels as independent. Figure 8 panel (c) is
the practical consequence: for three of the five classes the area estimate
depends more on which classifier is chosen than on anything the sampling
uncertainty admits, which is the reason the revised manuscript reports an
ensemble range rather than a single model's areas.
